# Page types

Choose the type from the intent (see research.md). Each type lists what it must contain and the evidence for it from our sites. Use the site's own design and components; these are content requirements, not layouts.

## Contents
1. Price answer
2. Free tool
3. Product or segment page
4. Service page
5. Industry page with case study
6. Local / area page
7. Guide
8. Comparison
9. Case study (work page)

## 1. Price answer

**When:** the query asks what something costs ("root canal cost in bangalore", "seo charges per month", "samsung s23 display price").

**Evidence:** A dental clinic's price list (308 clicks, 38.6k impressions, 3 months) and its "X Cost in Bangalore: From ₹N" treatment pages hold 17 #1 positions. A phone repair company's model-price posts. A billing-machine seller's product page with the one-time price in the title (position 2.2). Our SEO cost guide (#1–2 for Bangalore SEO price queries).

**Must contain:**
- H1 = the query. Title carries the answer: "X Cost in [City]: From ₹N" or "₹A–₹B".
- The price or range in the first two sentences.
- A price table (HTML table, not an image) with what is included.
- What changes the price, as a short list.
- Question headings for the cost questions people ask; FAQ schema.
- The date the prices were checked, and who set them (the client).
- A next step that fits the price (book, get a quote, WhatsApp).

**Watch:** prices must come from the client and be current. Old prices on a cost page damage trust fast. Add a review date to the brief.

## 2. Free tool

**When:** the searcher wants to do or check something ("schema checker", "phone test", "seo audit").

**Evidence:** 4 of a phone repair company's top 8 pages are phone-test tools at about 450 words; our [schema checker](https://www.socialscript.in/schema-audit) (35 clicks, 3,078 impressions).

**Must contain:** the tool above the fold; a short plain explanation of what it checks and how to read the result; question headings for the common follow-ups; a route from the result to our service ("fix these for me"). Keep the copy short; the tool is the content.

## 3. Product or segment page

**When:** the searcher wants a product for a situation ("billing machine for restaurants", "laptop on rent for students").

**Evidence:** A billing-machine seller's top 5 pages are segment collections that name the job ("Send KOTs to the kitchen. Keep tables turning.") with the price in the title and 11 question headings.

**Must contain:** H1 that names the job or situation; the price; the specifications that matter for that segment; FAQs for that segment; Product schema with offers; live stock or availability where possible.

## 4. Service page

**When:** the searcher wants a provider ("seo agency in bangalore", "web design company").

**Evidence:** our `/services/*` pages (300–460 words, no named proof) rank 27–66. Our Bangalore pages with named clients, prices and FAQs do better, but head terms are decided as much by authority, reviews and the map pack.

**Must contain:** what the service is in the client's words; who it is for; a price or price range (or "from"); named clients and links to case studies; what is included; process; FAQs; a CTA that asks for the conversation. Follow client-web-copy's B2B page structure.

**Also needed off the page:** Google Business Profile, reviews, directory profiles (Clutch, GoodFirms, Justdial as fits). Copy cannot do this part.

## 5. Industry page with case study

**When:** a business owner looks for marketing for their industry ("digital marketing for dental clinics").

**Evidence:** our export page has the best CTR on the site (6.3%). The dental page was built from this template with competitor and PAA research.

**Must contain:** a one-paragraph definition answer; a named case study with checkable, dated results and screenshots; how buyers in that industry decide; the channels, each answer-first; the industry's own rules (regulators, platform policies) with linked sources; pain points in the owner's words; services; FAQs from PAA; links to the other industry pages.

## 6. Local / area page

**When:** a service business wants searches from nearby areas.

**Evidence:** A dental clinic's templated area pages: 580 and 256 impressions, 0 clicks. A rage room directory's city pages win where demand exists because each has real prices, bookings and a map.

**Build only with real local content:** directions and travel time, local landmarks, what people from that area come for, reviews from that area, local prices if they differ. Otherwise fold the areas into one "areas we serve" page and let the Google Business Profile do the local work.

## 7. Guide

**When:** the searcher wants to understand something.

**Evidence:** 13 of our 36 blog posts are not indexed; all are general topics with no first-hand material and no sources. Our indexed guides are local and commercial and carry our own prices and process. A B2B trading company's generic guides are not indexed either.

**Build only if** we have something the ranking guides lack: our own data, prices, screenshots, client examples, a local angle, or a tested opinion. Must contain: the answer first; numbered steps or a table; a source for every number; links out to what it discusses; 5+ internal links in; a real author with an author page; the published and updated dates.

## 8. Comparison ("X vs Y")

Only with first-hand use of both, or real data. State who each option suits, a comparison table, and when not to choose either. Generic "X vs Y" posts are among the not-indexed pages on client sites.

## 9. Case study (work page)

Named client; what they needed in their words; what we did, specifically; results that are dated and checkable (rankings with the date checked, clicks from Search Console, leads only if the client agrees to publish them); screenshots; services used; links to the related industry and service pages. Ask the client before publishing numbers.
