# Research: demand, intent, competitors, results

Research decides whether a page is worth building and what it must contain. Most pages that never get indexed skipped this step.

## 1. Tools

- **Keyword data:** DataForSEO, Ahrefs, Semrush or Google Keyword Planner for volume, CPC and competition in the country or city you serve.
- **Live results:** a SERP API (DataForSEO's Google Organic endpoint returns the AI Overview and the domains it cites, People Also Ask and the map pack) or a manual search from the right location.
- **AI citations:** an AI visibility tool shows who AI answers cite for a topic, for example our free [AI visibility checker](https://www.socialscript.in/ai-visibility-checker).
- **Search Console** for 3-month page and query tables, index status, URL Inspection and sitemaps.
- **Firecrawl or web fetch** to read competitor pages.
- Subagents are useful for reading 6–10 competitor pages in parallel. Give them the URLs or titles and ask for type, word count, H2 outline, schema, proof, CTA and gaps, with source URLs.

## 2. Demand

- Get volume for the exact term and its variants. Sum the cluster: one page can serve "digital marketing for dental clinics", "dental digital marketing" and "dental marketing agency" if the intent is the same.
- **CPC is the commercial signal.** A term with low volume and a high CPC (dental marketing in India: 140 searches, $5.89) is worth more than a high-volume informational term.
- Use the right location. City-level codes exist (Bengaluru 1007768); India is 2356. Local service terms often show 0 at city level and real volume at country level; check both.
- Volume near zero does not always mean no page. A case study can still win clients and AI citations (our laptop rental page). Say plainly that it will not bring search traffic.

## 3. Intent

Write down, in one sentence, what the searcher wants to happen next. Then check:

- **Type:** price, provider, how-to, comparison, tool, product, local. The ranking pages tell you; if the top 10 are all guides, a bare service page will not rank.
- **Geography and currency.** A rupee price page answers Indian searchers. Worldwide head terms ("seo service price") will show it to people who will not click. Target the localised variants and accept the impressions on the rest as noise.
- **Stage.** Someone comparing providers needs proof and prices; someone learning needs an explanation first.

## 4. The live results page

From a SERP API or a manual search in the right location, note:

- What types of pages hold the top 10 (agency pages, guides, directories like Practo/Justdial/Clutch, tools, YouTube).
- **AI Overview:** present or not, and which domains it cites. If it cites YouTube, LinkedIn or directories, the page alone will not get us cited; plan the off-site piece too (video, LinkedIn posts, directory profiles).
- **People Also Ask:** these become question headings and FAQs. If a PAA question has no real answer (e.g. a "rule" with no source), say so plainly on the page; that honesty is itself citable.
- **Map pack:** if present, the Google Business Profile matters as much as the page.

## 5. Competitor pages and the gap

For the top 5–8 pages record: URL, type, word count, H2 outline, whether they are local, schema, proof (named clients, real numbers), CTA. Then write three lists:

- **Table stakes:** what every page covers. Ours must too.
- **Gaps:** what none covers well. For Indian pages these are often local rules and regulators, rupee prices, named local proof, local platforms (Practo, Justdial, IndiaMART), WhatsApp.
- **Formats the AI Overview lifts:** one-sentence definitions, numbered lists, tables.

Aim to be the most complete and the most specific page for the intent, not the longest. Our winners range from 450 words (a tool) to 2,400 (a treatment cost page).

## 6. Go / no-go

Build when all three hold:
1. There is demand with the right intent (or a stated non-search purpose).
2. We have first-hand material the ranking pages lack.
3. We can name the page type that wins for this intent.

If (2) fails, do not write it. Ask the client for the material or pick another topic.

## 7. Reading results after launch

Check at 2 and 6 weeks in Search Console.

| What you see | Likely cause | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| "Discovered/Crawled – currently not indexed" | Thin, generic or duplicate; few internal links | Add first-hand material, link from 5+ strong pages, or merge into a stronger page |
| Impressions, ~0 clicks, good position | Wrong intent or geography; AI Overview absorbs the click; weak title | Retarget the title to the localised intent; make the title carry the answer |
| Position 8–20 | Page is relevant but lacks authority or proof | More internal links, named proof, off-site citations (directories, reviews, video) |
| Position 1–5, clicks | Working | Protect its earning phrases; extend with related questions |
| Ranks for queries you did not target | Search Console shows real demand | Add a section or a new page for them |
