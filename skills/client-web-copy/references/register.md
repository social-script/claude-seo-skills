# Matching a client's register

## Why vocabulary beats claims

A buyer evaluating a supplier is running a fast, mostly unconscious test: does this company know my business? Vocabulary answers that faster than any claim, because terms of art cannot be bluffed. An exporter reading "MOQ" and "RCMC" concludes you have done this before. The same exporter reading "we deliver measurable results" concludes nothing.

This is why a page can be well written and still fail. Fluency is not familiarity.

## Building the vocabulary list

Aim for 20–30 real terms before drafting. Sources, in order of value:

1. **The client themselves.** Their existing site, a proposal, a quote, an email thread with a customer. This is the highest-value source and the most often skipped.
2. **The top 3 ranking pages** for the target term. Read them for language, not just structure.
3. **Regulators and trade bodies.** Council sites, government portals, certification bodies. These give you the formal names of things.
4. **Communities.** Forums, subreddits, LinkedIn posts from people in the trade — this is where you get how practitioners actually talk, which differs from the formal register.

Sort what you find into two buckets:

- **Table stakes** — terms any competent supplier uses. Using them gets you taken seriously. Missing them is disqualifying.
- **Insider** — terms that show you have done the work. Use a few, not all; over-stuffing reads as showing off.

## A worked example: Indian exporters

Table stakes: MOQ, HS code, IEC (Importer-Exporter Code), FOB/CIF, Incoterms, freight forwarder, customs clearance, letter of credit, proforma invoice.

Insider: RCMC, FIEO, APEDA / GJEPC / EPCH / EEPC (sector councils), Pharmexcil, duty drawback, RoDTEP, buyer-seller meet, ITPO.

The insider terms are what separate a page written for exporters from a page written about exporters.

## The rule that keeps you honest

**Do not use a term you could not define if asked.** A prospect on a call will ask, and the credibility you bought with the word is lost at several times the value. If you are unsure, either learn it properly or use the plain description instead.

## Adjacent registers

The same discipline applies outside trade:

- **Clinics** — patient acquisition, NABH/NABL, consultation-to-procedure conversion, panel empanelment, teleconsultation, footfall
- **Manufacturers** — RFQ, tooling, lead time, MOQ, tolerances, ISO 9001, capacity utilisation
- **Professional services** — engagement, scope, retainer, matter, billable, panel

## Register is not just words

Also match:

- **Sentence length.** Trade and industrial audiences read fast and skim. Short.
- **Formality.** Indian B2B skews more formal than US SaaS. "Enquiry" not "inquiry", "we would be glad to" over "let's chat".
- **Spelling.** Indian English follows British convention — organisation, specialised, programme, enquiry, licence (noun).
- **Currency and units.** ₹ with Indian digit grouping where relevant, metric throughout.
- **What counts as proof.** Certifications, registrations, years in business and named clients carry more weight than growth percentages.
